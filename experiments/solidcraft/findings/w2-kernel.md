# W2 (lane b) — kernel benchmark: SolidCraft's vendored-patched `truck` vs OCCT 8.0.1 (OCP)

- **Experiment:** `w2-kernel` (results: [`results/w2-kernel.json`](../results/w2-kernel.json) —
  predecessor's canonical measurements, plus an appended `verification_rerun` key; full re-run
  envelope + agreement analysis: [`results/w2-kernel-verification.json`](../results/w2-kernel-verification.json))
- **Owner:** W2 lane-b kernel-benchmark worker, Task ID 100, issue #1 candidate **#4 (kernel:
  truck vs OCCT/OCP)**. Provenance note: the predecessor agent built all artifacts (fixtures,
  both harnesses, orchestrator, result envelope) and died before documenting; this pass
  **verified the artifacts by re-running them** (§4) and wrote this document. Every number below
  is taken from the result JSONs — none invented.
- **Kernels compared:** truck as SolidCraft ships it — `solidcraft-kernel` at upstream pin
  `3b4db52c10279251a068cfca5eb7447068bc28e7` (v0.16.2) with the **vendored patched**
  truck-geometry/truck-shapeops/truck-meshalgo/truck-stepio (`[patch.crates-io]` replicated in
  the harness workspace) — vs **OCCT 8.0.1 through `cadquery-ocp` 8.0.1.1.0 wheels** in the
  experiment-side venv `.ocp-venv` (NOT a product dependency). Release-config truck evidence:
  the checksum-verified official binary `solidcraft-cli 0.16.2` (tag `5de48e9`, **80 commits
  behind the source pin** — 50 first-parent, 35 of them touching `crates/doc|engine|kernel`;
  recorded in every envelope header).
- **Verdict in one line:** both kernels are within the upstream oracle tolerance on **15/15**
  measurable fixtures and both refuse the same unbuildable case with clear errors, but OCCT is
  **1–2 orders of magnitude faster on curved-curved booleans** (confirmed in truck's *release*
  config, not just the dev harness) with exact surface integration and no wrong-result outcome
  on this set, while truck wins decisively on **packaging** (pure-Rust closure, C-runtime-only
  dynamic deps, ~20–47 MiB release processes vs OCP's ~290 MiB process baseline) and shows
  excellent STEP *export* fidelity — recommend **reference-only for the kernel decision
  (supports ADR-0002's OCCT direction) + adapt of specific design patterns**, see §6.

### 1.3 Comparability labeling policy

Every case carries a label + one-line reason (per-case `comparability` in the JSON). The
authoritative count — `fixtures/kernel/cases.json`, which the envelope's summary is computed
from — is **6 `comparable`** (k01 "planar box; both kernels judged against the closed-form
V=abc, A=2(ab+bc+ca)", k02 "analytic cylinder; truck measures tessellated, OCCT integrates
surfaces", k13 "analytic totals for the union", k14, k15, k38 "coincident lateral faces;
analytic result is one r10 h50 cylinder") and **10 `approximately-comparable`** (same part and
analytic totals, but by-design local geometry differs between kernels: k04 "seam/face splitting
differs by kernel", k07 "rolling-ball corners differ BY DESIGN between kernels; totals judged
against Steiner", k07b "no triple-edge corner patches; only end trimming can differ", k08
"chamfer miters at corners may differ", k09 "truck polyhedron::shell vs OCCT MakeThickSolid
are different algorithms; equal only on this convex analytic case", st1 "failure probe:
refusal with a clear error is an honest outcome", and — in `cases.json` — the four
robustness-success probes st2/st3/st4/st5). **Metadata inconsistency found and recorded:** the
executable mirrors (`w2_kernel.rs` fixture table, `ocp_kernel_bench.py` FIXTURES) label
st2/st3/st4/st5 `comparable` instead ("near-tangent cut with analytic total" etc.), i.e. 10/6
the other way round; `cases.json` is the declared source of truth and the summary follows it.
Verdicts are unaffected: robustness cases are judged by outcome class, and cross-kernel deltas
on them are recorded as observations either way. Cross-kernel deltas on approximately-comparable
cases are recorded as **observations, never verdicts** — the same policy as upstream's own
oracle README for FreeCAD-built corner shapes. Tolerance: the upstream oracle defaults,
`volume_rel = area_rel = 1e-3`, abs `1e-3 × bbox diagonal`. Truck measures **tessellated**
geometry (the same measurement the upstream oracle runner uses); OCCT integrates surfaces
exactly.

### 1.4 Honesty rules

Refusals are recorded **verbatim** and never dropped; a build that succeeded once and failed
later would be recorded verbatim (none occurred); wrong-volume STEP imports are flagged as
failures (the re-run's runner does this automatically — see §3.3); blocked measurements record
their blocker. Nothing was fabricated; the predecessor's numbers are canonical and were
preserved byte-for-byte (§4).

## 2. Kernel-quality audit notes (source-backed)

### 2.1 What the vendored truck patches mean (`/home/z/solidcraft-upstream/vendor/README.md`, read completely)

SolidCraft's shipped kernel is **not stock truck** — it is a maintained fork of 4 truck crates
(+ matext4cgmath, egui-wgpu), every change marked `SolidCraft:` in source, Apache-2.0 © RICOS
retained (license audit: Task 101 / `findings/w3b-supply-chain.md`). Four families of patches:

- **`truck-shapeops` 0.4.0 — boolean robustness + NEW history.** Rejects empty/single-point
  polylines and collapsed/non-finite intersection segments before indexing; meshes both shells
  with `robust_triangulation` when integrating (post-boolean boundaries lie on their surface
  only within tolerance); falls back to nearest parameter when exact projection fails; **result
  face/shell order made deterministic** (truck's connected-components followed hash order of
  face addresses, "so a boolean's faces came out in a different order on every run"); a point
  within point-tolerance of a boundary-curve end *is* that end (#391: an intersection curve
  ending 2e-7 mm from another's end used to cut a sliver and leave the shell open);
  vertex-visited-by-multiple-wires ordering by swept angle (`add_edge_by_angle`); non-simple
  face wires make the operation **give up instead of panicking**; `tr!` tracing. And the
  headline for us: **`transversal/history.rs` is NEW** — `and_with_history`/`or_with_history`
  return, per result face and edge, the operand face/edge it came from (or the two operand
  faces whose intersection made it), recorded *during construction, never reconstructed*. This
  is the substrate W2-a audited for persistent naming (Task 98): face/edge provenance for
  booleans, which stock truck does not provide.
- **`truck-geometry` 0.5.0 — math + perf correctness.** `IntersectionCurve::parameter_division`
  now divides with hinted projections from the previous point instead of a whole-surface grid
  pre-search per point (a small hole on a large curved face "took seconds", #1038 — regression
  test `crates/kernel/src/issue_1038_tests.rs`); revolved-surface seam handling picks the
  nearer period end (seam parameter polygon could fold over itself; test `seam_faces_tests.rs`);
  **`uuder`/`vvder` multiplied by p instead of p·(p−1)**, so every degree-≥3 B-spline/NURBS
  surface's second derivatives were 1/(p−1) of their true value — a cubic surface's curvature
  read as half (#763; test `surface_second_derivative_tests.rs` holds them to finite
  differences). These are real upstream math bugs fixed in-tree.
- **`truck-meshalgo` 0.4.0 — robustness/bounded-work series (hostile files must not hang).**
  Bounded divisions (≤20 000 points/edge, 10 M points/shell grid budget, 24 refinement
  rounds); hinted-search failures retried unhinted (#882); `try_subs` instead of panicking
  `subs` (#1252); pole-parameter corruption at collapsed edges (#796); twisted ruled faces
  split by plane-bow (#1666); shell-wide shared edge points preventing mesh cracks;
  **deterministic collision order with old-vs-new equivalence tests** (`mesh_collision_equivalence.rs`,
  `tess_include_equivalence.rs` behind test-hooks features). Tessellation for *display* gains
  angle-bounded deflection; a bounded provisional `preview_face` path exists for the kernel.
- **`truck-stepio` 0.3.0 — writer-only (SolidCraft reads STEP with its own reader).**
  Duplicate-entity-id fix (an `INTERSECTION_CURVE`'s second surface was written at the first's
  entity index — "every boolean-made edge produced duplicate entity ids"); correct
  `same_sense` for inverted/mirrored surfaces of revolution; transform-commuting revolutions
  written exactly; export errors reported instead of panicking.

**Meaning for kernel quality:** the kernel under test is a disciplined, documented fork with
its own in-tree regression suite keyed to numbered issues, not a casual vendoring. The
consequences for us are two-sided: the fork demonstrably fixes real correctness/robustness
defects (including one that makes booleans deterministic — a precondition for the persistent
naming of Task 98), but adopting it means inheriting fork-maintenance burden against upstream
truck (drift risk; patches are `SolidCraft:`-marked but not upstreamed as far as the vendor
README states).

### 2.2 The kernel crate's own API surface (pin `3b4db52`, paths + line counts)

- **`crates/kernel/src/body.rs`** (1,723 lines): `Body` is an `Arc`-shared immutable
  `mt::Solid` (or a triangle-only mesh body for imported 3MF/STL) with cached bbox diagonal
  ("every kernel tolerance is a fraction of it"), a shared cached volume (a boolean measures
  its operands; later booleans re-measure), a reusable coarse missing-face check, and
  display-only paint/split metadata that operations drop. Immutable-by-construction composes
  with our transactions-only document mutation.
- **`crates/kernel/src/build.rs`** (2,356 lines): extrude/revolve of planar `Region2`s and
  primitives, with bounded input (`MAX_SEGS = 20_000` per loop) and finite-value validation
  (`finite()` rejects |x| ≥ 1e7) — inputs are refused loudly, not truncated.
- **`crates/kernel/src/boolean_guard.rs`** (218 lines, issue #616): **every Boolean retry's
  result must pass a verification guard before it is returned**: closed; a join never loses
  volume and a cut never gains any; and outside the tool's bounding box the result's surface
  must equal the target's (sampled ≤20 000 triangles through a uniform grid). "A wrong result
  that looks right is worse" — the crate refuses rather than returns a plausible-looking
  wrong solid. This is a kernel-agnostic **design pattern** worth adapting whatever kernel we
  use (see §6).
- **`crates/kernel/src/blend.rs`** (2,877 lines): fillets and chamfers are implemented as a
  **local, exact B-rep operation**, not a general rolling-ball blend: "a straight edge between
  two planar faces whose two end vertices each touch exactly one more planar face; fillets
  require perpendicular end faces… The result is exact. Edges meeting at an already blended
  corner, curved edges and other configurations are reported as not supported yet." This
  bounds truck-side fillet coverage relative to OCCT's general `BRepFilletAPI` and explains
  the st1 refusal wording (§3.2).

### 3.2 Failure / robustness outcomes (verbatim)

- **st1-fillet-too-large (fillet r=200 on a 40×30×20 box), truck:**
  `the operation failed: not supported yet: the blend is larger than the neighbouring faces; the largest radius that fits is 10 mm`

  on either side on this fixture set.
- **Third failure record (added by the re-run's runner detection, numbers identical in both

### 3.3 STEP round-trip and cross-kernel reads

**Within-kernel round-trips** (15 files each side): truck export (vendored stepio, schema
`AP242_MANAGED_MODEL_BASED_3D_ENGINEERING_MIM_LF`) → truck import: rel volume delta ≤
1.21e-4 (max st5 1.21e-4; curved cases typically 2–5e-5, e.g. k38 5.2e-5, k13 2.4e-5;
k08/k09/st3/st4 exact to 0.0 or 4e-16; st2 round-trips as 2 closed bodies — both kernels
agree on the split). OCP write → OCP read: ≤ 1.36e-10 (mostly ~1e-14). Both clean: no
warnings, all bodies closed.

**Cross-read, truck writes → OCP reads (15/15):** rel volume deltas **≤ 5.7e-7** vs the OCP
side's own analytic-exact measures (k15 5.7e-7 is the max; k07 1.8e-10; most ≤ 1e-15), all
shapes valid. Notable: OCP reading *truck's* STEP reproduces the **analytic** volumes (k02:
35342.91735288519 vs analytic 35342.917352885175; k13: 45571.68026350791) even where truck's
internal tessellated measure reads ~1e-4 low (35338.63 / 45569.18). **Truck's exported B-rep
geometry is exact; its ~1e-4 measurement deltas are tessellation artifacts of
`kernel::measure`** (the same measurement the upstream oracle runner uses), not geometry error.

**Cross-read, OCP writes → truck reads (15/15 files imported, all closed, zero warnings):**
rel volume deltas vs truck's own direct-API measures: 0.0 on k01/k08/k09/st3/st4;
1.8e-6…1.5e-4 on the curved cases (k14 1.45e-4, k38 5.4e-5, st2 5.5e-5 — same
tessellated-measurement noise class); **one real anomaly: st5-near-tangent-sphere-union
rel 4.11e-2** — truck's own STEP reader measures the OCCT-written near-tangent lens union as
**8722.072655 mm³ vs 8377.58 analytic** (+4.1%; area +6.0%), while OCP reading *truck's* st5
STEP gets 8377.578839039288 (rel 1.4e-10) and truck's direct API builds it correctly
(8377.43, rel 1.8e-5). The mis-read body is closed, 2 faces, no warnings — a *plausible
looking* wrong volume on a near-tangent sphere-sphere import. The re-run's runner flags this
automatically as `step-crossread-volume-anomaly` (`failures[2]`); the predecessor envelope
contains the identical numbers but predates the flagging code. Recorded as an honest truck-side
robustness gap on the STEP **import** path (SolidCraft's own reader, not the vendored stepio
writer) for near-tangent curved-curved intersections.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Dev-vs-release profile for direct-API truck measurements.** Correctness, STEP and
   in-process timings come from the dev-profile harness (deps opt-level 2, debug-assertions
   off — upstream's own dev profile). The release-config evidence is the official CLI (timing,
   oracle, memory) and it *confirms* the boolean timings and shows much lower st5 memory —
   but per-op in-process dev numbers should not be quoted as product performance.
2. **Single-host sandbox:** 2 CPUs, ~4 GiB cgroup, shared with other processes (STOP/CONT
   discipline around builds). No multi-host spread, no statistics beyond min/mean/max over 3–5
   repeats; absolute ms values are indicative, ratios more robust.
3. **80-commit pin-vs-binary drift:** behavioural CLI evidence is the official tag-`5de48e9`
   binary; source audits and the direct-API harness are at pin `3b4db52` (35 of the 80 commits
   touch `crates/doc|engine|kernel`). The two truck paths could diverge in principle; the
   oracle replay (10/10 pass) and volume agreement between CLI and harness measures make this
   low-risk here, but it is a real provenance caveat recorded in every envelope header.
4. **Tessellation excluded from verdicts:** the vendored meshalgo patches add display-mesh
   policies (angle-bounded deflection, preview paths) that this benchmark does not exercise —
   display-only for us; a future evaluation should cover meshing if it becomes a product path.
5. **Fixture scale:** 16 small parts (≤ 112 500 mm³, ≤ 26 faces). No large-assembly, no
   deep-feature-tree, no spline-heavy, no imported-STEP-hostile corpus (truck's
   bounded-work series targets those). The st5 anomaly shows at least one class where a
   single near-tangent probe found a 4% STEP-import error — a wider hostile corpus could find
   more on either side.
6. **Measurement asymmetry:** truck measures tessellated, OCCT integrates surfaces — the
   ~1e-4 truck deltas are an artifact of that (proven by §3.3 cross-reads), but it also means
   truck-side *area/CoM* precision claims must stay at the 1e-3 tolerance level.
7. **Self-authored expectations** (6 cases) are self-derived analytic forms, checked against a
   second kernel, but not against an independent oracle (upstream's FreeCAD oracle covers only
   its own 83 cases).

## 6. Recommendation input for candidate #4 (truck vs OCCT/OCP) — for TL2's ADR-level decision

**Recommendation: REFERENCE-ONLY for the kernel decision (the evidence supports ADR-0002's
OCCT product direction), plus ADAPT of specific kernel-agnostic design patterns.** Not adopt,
not port, not reject — and not "insufficient evidence" for this posture (the evidence is
decision-grade for *reference*; it would be insufficient for a *switch*, which is exactly the
point).

**Why the evidence supports keeping OCCT (ADR-0002):**

- **Performance on the CAD hot path:** curved-curved booleans are 1–2 orders faster in OCCT
  (k15 intersect 29.3 ms vs 1041.8 ms *release-config*; k14 cut 3.4 vs 298.7; st5 6.3 vs
  481.6), at parity only on planar/coincident cases. Booleans are the hot path ADR-0002
  names first.
- **Robustness edge found, honestly:** truck's STEP import mis-reads an OCCT-written
  near-tangent sphere union by +4.1% volume with a closed, warning-free body (§3.3) — the
  "wrong result that looks right" their own boolean_guard.rs philosophy says is worst; OCP
  showed no wrong-result outcome in either cross-read direction on this set.
- **Measurement precision:** OCCT integrates surfaces exactly (0.0 to 8e-9 rel on every
  measurable case); truck's tessellated measure tops out at ~1.5e-4 — fine at the 1e-3 oracle
  tolerance, weaker for precision-verification features.
- **Memory (mixed, profile-dependent — stated honestly):** in the dev-profile harness truck
  spikes to 699.9 MiB on st5 (2.4× OCP's whole-process 291 MiB peak), but the **release-config
  CLI peaks at 47.1 MiB on the identical input** vs OCP's flat ~290 MiB process baseline
  (python + OCCT + TBB + FFMpeg mapped). The dev spike is evidence of allocation-heavy
  near-tangent boolean code, not of shipped footprint; neither side dominates this axis
  outright once profiles are matched.
- **Packaging is the one axis where truck wins decisively** (pure-Rust closure, C-runtime-only
  dynamic deps, no 45–65 MB-per-platform C++ wheel chain) — but our ADR-0002 already accepts
  the OCCT wheel weight (OCP is Apache-2.0, wheels proven; `kernel-bindings-evaluation-m1.md`).

**What is genuinely worth ADAPTING from the truck side, kernel-agnostically:**

- the **boolean result-verification guard** pattern (`crates/kernel/src/boolean_guard.rs`:
  closure + volume monotonicity + outside-tool-box invariance, refuse-on-fail) — implementable
  as a product-side checker over OCCT results;
- the **refusal semantics / error-message bar** ("the largest radius that fits is 10 mm" vs
  `IsDone() is false`);
- **face/edge provenance recording during construction** (`vendor/truck-shapeops/transversal/history.rs`)
  as the design reference for our persistent-naming needs — with the OCCT analogue
  (`BRepBuilderAPI` Modified/Generated history) to be evaluated in our own ADR;
- the **oracle-style analytic benchmark discipline** (fixture set + verbatim refusal recording
  + cross-kernel STEP reads) as the template for our own kernel regression suite;
- **bounded-work policies** (hostile inputs must not hang) as a product requirement whatever
  kernel we use.

**What would have to change for truck to become adoption-credible (the bar for any future
kernel ADR, per issue #1 boundary #5 — a separately reviewed ADR with):** parity/performance
evidence at scale (large parts, deep trees, spline-heavy and hostile imported-STEP corpora);
reliability parity on degenerate inputs **including its own STEP import path** (fix or bound
the near-tangent mis-read class); packaging/ABI story for our Python extension runtime;
topology-identity requirements (history.rs provenance vs OCCT Modified/Generated) mapped to our
persistent-naming design; and a regression suite equivalent to upstream's 83-case oracle run
against our own fixture corpus. **This document does not propose changing the production
kernel choice** — ADR-0002 keeps OCCT as the product direction and this evidence is input to
that decision, not an implementation of any change (issue #1 boundary #5: no production
kernel/contract changes from the evaluation track).
